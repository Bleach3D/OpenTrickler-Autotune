Powder Profile Tuning Guide
Select Profile
Changing the selected profile saves it as the reboot default.
Controller PID is the original OpenTrickler control loop and is recommended for normal use - it takes its stop points directly from your thresholds below. Adaptive is the legacy controller from Opentrickler_ML that computes its own runtime stop margins; kept for reference/compatibility, not recommended.
Legacy Adaptive Controller Options
These two only affect the legacy adaptive controller. They do nothing while the Controller above is set to PID, because the PID path takes its stop points straight from your thresholds.
Coarse Stop Authority Whether the legacy adaptive controller may choose its own coarse handoff point instead of using your threshold. No effect while the Controller is set to PID. "Use my threshold" caps how much the AI leaves for the fine tube at handoff, so it can't quietly hand off several grains early. It is still raised automatically if the coarse motor physically needs more room to stop without overshooting — the live status line reports when that happens. "Let AI tuning decide" restores the fully adaptive behaviour.
Accuracy vs Speed Shifts how much finishing margin the legacy adaptive controller leaves the fine phase. No effect while the Controller is set to PID.
Faster Balanced More accurate
Shifts how much finishing margin the fine phase is given. Toward "faster" it finishes sooner with a little more spread; toward "more accurate" it works to a tighter finish and takes longer. Centre is neutral and matches previous behaviour.
Powder Kernel Weight grains
-
Weight of a single powder granule ("kernel"), in grains. AI Tuning estimates this automatically from characterization, but entering a value you measured yourself is more reliable and always overrides the estimate. Used as the floor for Fine Trickler Stop Threshold and for Manual Finish's suggested kernel count below. The same value is shown and edited here and on the AI Tuning page — there is only one stored value per profile.
Neopixel LED Normal Charge Colour Ring colour when the finished charge is inside your Accepted Charge Tolerance.
Neopixel LED Under Charge Colour Ring colour when the finished charge is below tolerance.
Neopixel LED Over Charge Colour Ring colour when the finished charge is above tolerance. Worth choosing something unmissable — an overcharge is the one you must not load.
Neopixel LED Not Ready Colour Ring colour while the system is not ready, for example waiting for the pan or for a stable zero.
Coarse Trickler Stop Threshold grains How much of the charge is left for the fine tube when the coarse tube stops. Typical 0.5–1.5 gn. Lower = coarse runs longer, faster throws, but more risk it overshoots on its own momentum. Higher = safer against overshoot, but the fine tube has more work so throws take longer and vary more. If throws are slow, lower this first. Must stay above the coarse tube's measured tail, or the coarse tube will carry past target after it stops.
Accepted Charge Tolerance ± grains The ± band that decides whether a finished charge is accepted (green) or flagged under/over. This is also the band the controller works to: it keeps topping up while the charge is more than this under target, so it will not hand you a short charge and call it done. Typical 0.02–0.04 gn. Lower = tighter charges, longer throws. Higher = faster, looser. Cannot go finer than about half a scale count (FX-120i resolves ~0.0154 gn).
Manual Finish
For large-kernel powders (e.g. N565/N570-class extruded stick powder, ~0.08gn per kernel) where your Accepted Charge Tolerance is tighter than one whole kernel. The trickler cannot safely add another full kernel without risking an overcharge, so instead of guessing it stops, blinks the under-charge LED, and tells you how many hand-cut partial kernels to drop in. It then verifies and goes green or red exactly like an automatic finish.
Manual Finish Needs a known kernel weight for this profile — either AI-estimated from characterization or entered by hand on the AI Tuning page. Without one, this setting has nothing to compare against and does nothing.
Cut Kernel Weight grains The weight of one hand-cut fragment. If a whole kernel is 0.08gn and you cut it in half, enter 0.04; cut into quarters, enter 0.02. The suggested count shown on the trickler and here is simply the remaining gap divided by this number, rounded to the nearest whole fragment. Leave at 0 to fall back to suggesting whole kernels, which only tells you the gap rather than helping close it.

Reverse Tube
After a tube's own final stop for this charge, briefly runs that motor backwards to pull the last bit of powder back out of the tube/gate instead of letting it keep dribbling in after the scale has already been read. Configured separately for the coarse and fine tubes since they hold very different amounts of residual powder. Has no effect on aborted charges.
Coarse Tube Reverse Reverses the coarse motor once, right after it makes its own final stop for the charge.
Coarse Reverse Amount revolutions How far to run the coarse motor backwards, in motor revolutions. Typical 0.05–0.3. Runs at the tube's own configured Coarse Trickler Min Flow Speed. Too much can pull powder back past the gate; start small and increase only if you still see dribble.
Fine Tube Reverse Reverses the fine motor once, right after it makes its own final stop for the charge (including a successful Manual Finish).
Fine Reverse Amount revolutions How far to run the fine motor backwards, in motor revolutions. Typical 0.02–0.15 — the fine tube usually needs much less than the coarse tube. Runs at the tube's own configured Fine Trickler Min Flow Speed. Too much can pull powder back past the gate; start small.
Fine Trickler Stop Threshold grains How close to target counts as done for the fine tube — it stops once the remaining weight is under this. Typical 0.02–0.05 gn. Lower = tighter charges but more time creeping, and more chance of tipping into an overcharge. Higher = quicker, consistently a touch light. Do not set below your powder kernel weight — asking for 0.01 gn from a 0.04 gn granule is asking for something the hardware cannot deliver.
Scale Settle — Stability (SD) grains Not an accuracy target despite the old name. This is how still the scale must be before a reading is trusted: the standard deviation across the last few readings must fall below it. Typical 0.01–0.03 gn. Lower = waits for a calmer scale, slower but more repeatable. Higher = accepts readings sooner, faster but noise can be mistaken for powder. Raise it on a draughty or vibrating bench; lower it if results scatter.
Scale Settle — Zero Band (Mean) grains How close to zero the pan must average before the system accepts it as tared and ready. Typical 0.02–0.05 gn. Lower insists on a truer zero but can hang at "waiting for zero" if the scale drifts. Higher starts sooner but carries a small offset into the charge. This does not decide whether a finished charge is good — that is Accepted Charge Tolerance.
Decimal Places Display resolution on the device screen. Cosmetic only — it does not change control precision. An FX-120i resolves about 0.0154 gn, so the third decimal is finer than the scale can actually see.
Enable Pre-Charge (requires servo gate) Uses the servo gate to dump most of the charge quickly before trickling starts. Needs a servo gate fitted. Big time saver on heavy charges; leave off if you do not have the hardware.
Pre-Charge Duration (ms) How long the gate stays open dumping bulk powder. Longer = more delivered fast and quicker throws, but if the dump overshoots target the charge is ruined. Start short and increase while checking the dump still lands well short of target — ideally leaving a bit more than the coarse stop threshold.
Pre-Charge Speed (rps) Motor speed used during the pre-charge dump. Higher moves powder faster but makes the dump amount less repeatable. Adjust the duration first and treat this as a coarse control.
Mid-stage Servo Gate Position (0=open, 1=close) Where the servo gate sits during the main trickling stages, 0 fully open to 1 fully closed. Partly closing restricts flow for a steadier trickle. Tune by eye: enough opening to keep powder moving without surging.
Auto-Zero Scale on Cup Return Automatically tares the scale when you put the empty pan back. Saves a button press each cycle. Turn off if your pan seats inconsistently, since an auto-tare on a badly seated pan puts a hidden offset into the next charge.
Pulse Mode
Enable Pulse Mode Instead of running the fine tube continuously near target, run it in short bursts with pauses so the scale can settle between them. Very useful on slow-settling scales such as the FX-120i, where continuous running means you are always reacting to a reading roughly a second out of date. Costs a little time. Pulses motor near target instead of continuous run - helps slow scales
Pulse Threshold (grains) How close to target pulsing begins, in grains remaining. Typical 0.3–0.6 gn. Higher = starts pulsing earlier, gentler and more accurate but slower. Lower = runs continuously for longer, quicker but relies on stale readings closer to target. Start pulsing when within this weight of target (0.3-1.0 gr)
Pulse Duration (ms) How long the motor runs in each pulse. This is effectively how much powder one pulse delivers. Aim for roughly one to two kernels per pulse — with a 0.04 gn powder and a fine tube doing ~0.15 gn/s, that is around 300–500 ms. Too long and each pulse can overshoot; too short and the tube may not reliably start turning. Motor on time per pulse
Pulse Wait (ms) Pause between pulses, giving the scale time to settle so the next decision uses a real reading rather than powder still in flight. Too short is the classic cause of overshoot in pulse mode. On an FX-120i start around 250–400 ms. Raise it if you overshoot in pulse mode; lower it only if the finish feels needlessly slow. Wait time for scale to update between pulses
Scale Stabilization
Fixed Stabilization Wait How long to wait after the motors stop before reading the final weight. Fixed waits a set time every throw — predictable and simple. Adaptive watches the reading and continues once it is genuinely stable, which is usually quicker but varies throw to throw. ON = fixed wait time; OFF = adaptive SD-based (up to 3s)
Stabilization Time (ms) The fixed settling wait, used only when the mode above is set to Fixed. Longer = more trustworthy final figure, slower cycle. On an FX-120i, under about 1000 ms risks recording a value the scale has not finished settling on. Fixed wait after motors stop (used when fixed stabilization is ON)
PID Gains
Only used while the Controller above is set to PID — the recommended and default path. Switching to the legacy Adaptive controller computes its own runtime margins instead.
Profile Name A name for this powder/load combination, up to 15 characters. Each profile keeps its own PID gains, speeds, thresholds and AI model, so use one profile per powder.
Coarse Trickler Kp How hard the coarse tube reacts to how far you are from target. Speed = Kp × remaining weight. Suggested value comes from measured flow: Kp = 1/(τ·k). Raise for a faster, more aggressive coarse run; too high and it slams to max speed then overshoots the handoff. Lower for a gentler ramp that costs a few seconds. Symptom of too high: coarse repeatedly blows past the stop threshold. Symptom of too low: coarse crawls and the throw drags.
Coarse Trickler Ki Integral term — accumulates leftover error over time to grind out a steady offset. Suggested 0, and 0 is usually right. The coarse phase is short and ends well short of target, so there is no steady-state error worth correcting, and any wind-up carries straight into an overshoot. Only add a very small value (0.001–0.01) if coarse consistently stalls short of the handoff.
Coarse Trickler Kd Derivative term — reacts to how fast the weight is changing. Suggested 0, and 0 is strongly recommended on an A&D FX-120i. The scale quantises to ~0.0154 gn, so the rate signal is mostly quantisation steps; a derivative term amplifies that into speed jitter. Leave at 0 unless you have a much faster, finer scale.
Coarse Trickler Min Flow Speed (rps) Slowest the coarse tube is allowed to turn. Below a certain speed a stepper stalls or delivers erratically, so this is a floor, not a target. Raise if the coarse tube stutters or stalls near the end of its run. Lower for a gentler approach to handoff. Suggested value is the measured trim speed from characterization.
Coarse Trickler Max Flow Speed (rps) Fastest the coarse tube may turn. This sets how quickly bulk powder is delivered and is the main lever on total throw time. Raise for faster throws, but a faster tube has more momentum and a bigger tail, so the coarse stop threshold must grow to match. Lower if you see overshoot past the handoff that raising the stop threshold cannot fix.
Fine Trickler Kp How hard the fine tube reacts to remaining weight. This is the gain that decides your final accuracy and the length of the last few seconds. Raise to close the last fraction of a grain quicker, at the risk of overshooting past target. Lower for a slower, safer creep. If you regularly go over by a small amount, lower this before touching anything else.
Fine Trickler Ki Integral term for the fine tube. Suggested 0. The fine phase is the long one, so this is where wind-up does real damage: error accumulates while the scale lags, then dumps extra powder just as you reach target. Leave at 0 unless the fine tube reliably stalls just short of target, and then only 0.001–0.005.
Fine Trickler Kd Derivative term for the fine tube. Suggested 0 and best left there. Same reason as coarse Kd, but worse: near target the real weight change per reading is around one scale count, so a derivative term is reacting almost entirely to quantisation noise.
Fine Trickler Min Flow Speed (rps) Slowest the fine tube may turn — effectively your finest possible delivery rate, and therefore a hard limit on accuracy. Lower for finer control right at target, until the motor starts stalling or delivering in unpredictable clumps. Raise if the last stretch takes too long or the tube stalls.
Fine Trickler Max Flow Speed (rps) Fastest the fine tube may turn. Sets how quickly the gap left by the coarse tube is closed. Raise to shorten the fine phase, but the tail grows with speed so the fine stop threshold must grow too. Lower for a calmer finish. If throws are slow, raising the coarse stop threshold usually helps more than raising this.
